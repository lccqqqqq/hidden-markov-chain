# **Problem: Token embeddings from co-occurrence via a statistical–mechanical limit**

## 1. Setup

* Vocabulary size: (V) (finite).
* Consider a sequence of tokens (w_1, \dots, w_L) drawn from a stationary stochastic process.
* Define window counts:
  [
  N_i = #{t \in {1,\dots,L} : w_t = i}, \quad \sum_i N_i = L.
  ]

Goal:

* Understand how **low-dimensional token embeddings** arise from co-occurrence statistics.
* Derive a principled notion of embedding from the **probability distribution over large context windows**.

---

## 2. Large-window limit and Gaussian structure

Assume:

* The token process is stationary and mixing (finite correlation length).

Define centered fluctuations:
[
X_i = \frac{N_i - L p_i}{\sqrt{L}}, \quad p_i = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{w=i}].
]

Then as (L \to \infty):
[
X ;\Rightarrow; \mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma),
]
subject to (\sum_i X_i = 0).

Where:
[
\Sigma_{ij}
===========

\sum_{\tau=-\infty}^{\infty}
\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{1}*{w_0=i}, \mathbf{1}*{w_\tau=j}).
]

Interpretation:

* (\Sigma) is the **integrated connected two-point function**.
* The Gaussian is the **maximum-entropy distribution consistent with all pairwise correlations**.

---

## 3. Relation to co-occurrence matrices

Define:
[
C_{ij}^{(L)} = \mathbb{E}[N_i N_j].
]

Then:
[
C_{ij}^{(L)} = L^2 p_i p_j + L \Sigma_{ij} + o(L).
]

Thus:

* Leading term: rank-1 (frequency structure).
* Nontrivial structure: in the **connected covariance**
  [
  \widetilde{C}*{ij} = \mathrm{Cov}(N_i, N_j) \sim L \Sigma*{ij}.
  ]

Conclusion:

* The meaningful object for embeddings is **(\Sigma)** (or a normalized variant), not raw counts.

---

## 4. Embeddings from the Gaussian theory

Diagonalize:
[
\Sigma = U \Lambda U^T.
]

Define token embeddings:
[
e_i = (\sqrt{\lambda_a}, U_{ia})_{a=1}^{V-1}.
]

Then:
[
\Sigma_{ij} = e_i \cdot e_j.
]

Interpretation:

* Embeddings are **coordinates of tokens in the principal fluctuation modes**.
* Each coordinate corresponds to a **collective pattern of co-occurrence**.

---

## 5. Gaussian effective theory in embedding space

Write:
[
X_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{t=1}^L (\mathbf{1}_{w_t=i} - p_i).
]

Define features:
[
\phi_a(i) = \frac{U_{ia}}{\sqrt{\lambda_a}}.
]

Then define global variables:
[
Q_a = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{t=1}^L \phi_a(w_t).
]

The Gaussian probability becomes:
[
P \propto \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \sum_a Q_a^2 \right).
]

Key fact:

> The Gaussian theory depends only on **additive sums of token embeddings over the window**.

---

## 6. Interpretation

* The Gaussian approximation captures:

  * all **pairwise correlations**,
  * integrated over the entire window,
  * but not ordering or higher-order interactions.

* Embeddings provide a representation in which:

  * the leading-order statistics are **fully captured by linear features**,
  * i.e. by sums of embedding vectors.

---

## 7. Role of SVD / PCA

* SVD of co-occurrence (or PMI-like matrices) corresponds to diagonalizing (\Sigma).
* Truncating to top (k) modes gives the best rank-(k) approximation:
  [
  \Sigma \approx E_k E_k^T.
  ]

Thus embeddings arise as:

* a **low-rank factorization of the Gaussian covariance**,
* capturing dominant global correlations.

---

## 8. Infinite-range vs Gaussianity

* After coarse-graining, the effective action contains:
  [
  \sum_{t,s} \phi(w_t)\cdot \phi(w_s),
  ]
  i.e. **all-to-all (infinite-range) interactions** across the window.

However:

* Gaussianity does **not** follow from infinite-range interactions.
* It follows from a **central limit theorem**:

  * counts are sums of many weakly correlated contributions,
  * yielding Gaussian fluctuations.

Thus:

> The Gaussian theory is asymptotically exact when the underlying process has short-range correlations and (L \to \infty).

---

## 9. What remains beyond embeddings

The Gaussian theory omits:

* **ordering / positional structure**
* **higher cumulants**:
  [
  \kappa^{(3)}, \kappa^{(4)}, \dots
  ]
* **multi-token interactions**

These correspond to:

* deviations from Gaussianity,
* which must be learned by more expressive models.

---

## 10. Conceptual conclusion

> In the large-context-window limit, the distribution of token counts becomes Gaussian and is fully characterized by a covariance matrix (\Sigma). Token embeddings arise naturally as a square-root factorization of (\Sigma), providing coordinates in which the leading-order (Gaussian) statistics depend only on additive features of tokens. This makes embeddings the natural first representation for learning, with subsequent modeling focused on non-Gaussian corrections and ordering effects.

all second moments of the count vector,

6. Interpretation
The Gaussian approximation captures exactly the leading-order statistics of long-window bag-of-words fluctuations:

all second moments of the count vector,

What the construction learns is therefore not “meaning” in the full linguistic sense, but the dominant second-order fluctuation geometry of token counts over large contexts.

\begin{quote}
\noindent\textbf{Roadmap.}
Consider a large context window and forget token order, keeping only the count vector \(N\). Since \(N\) is a sum of many weakly dependent one-hot token indicators, short-range dependence implies a multivariate central limit theorem: the centered fluctuations \((N-Lp)/\sqrt L\) become Gaussian. The leading-order statistics of large windows are therefore encoded by a single matrix, the long-run covariance \(\Sigma\). A factorization \(\Sigma = EE^\top\) then gives token vectors whose inner products reproduce these fluctuations. In this sense, embeddings arise as coordinates for the dominant collective modes of co-occurrence. Low-dimensional embeddings appear when \(\Sigma\) is well-approximated by its top eigenspaces; ordering and higher-order interactions enter only beyond this Gaussian level.
\end{quote}

\section{Setup}

Let \(\mathcal V = \{1,\dots,V\}\) be a finite vocabulary, and let \((w_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary token process. For a contiguous window of length \(L\), define the count vector
\[
N = (N_1,\dots,N_V)^\top,
\qquad
N_i = \#\{t\in\{1,\dots,L\}: w_t = i\},
\]
so that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^V N_i = L.
\]

Introduce the one-hot process
\[
Y_t \in \mathbb R^V,
\qquad
(Y_t)_i = \mathbf{1}_{\{w_t=i\}},
\]
so that
\[
N = \sum_{t=1}^L Y_t.
\]
Let
\[
p = \mathbb E[Y_t],
\qquad
p_i = \mathbb P(w_t=i).
\]

We want to understand what notion of token embedding is naturally implied by the distribution of large context windows.

\section{Long-window Gaussian limit}

Assume the process is stationary with short-range dependence, in the sense that a multivariate CLT holds for additive observables and the covariance series is summable. Define the lag-\(\tau\) covariance matrices
\[
\Gamma(\tau) = \operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_\tau),
\]
and the long-run covariance
\[
\Sigma = \sum_{\tau\in\mathbb Z} \Gamma(\tau).
\]

Now define the centered, rescaled count fluctuations
\[
X_L = \frac{N-Lp}{\sqrt L}.
\]
Then
\[
X_L \Rightarrow \mathcal N_H(0,\Sigma),
\]
where the Gaussian is supported on the hyperplane
\[
H = \{x\in\mathbb R^V : \mathbf 1^\top x = 0\}.
\]

The support restriction is forced by the exact identity \(\mathbf 1^\top N = L\), hence \(\mathbf 1^\top X_L = 0\). Accordingly,
\[
\Sigma \mathbf 1 = 0,
\qquad
\mathbf 1^\top \Sigma = 0.
\]

Thus, at leading order in large windows, the probability law of token counts is Gaussian and completely determined by \(\Sigma\).

\section{Window covariances and the meaning of \(\Sigma\)}

The covariance of the window counts satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(N)
=
\sum_{s,t=1}^L \operatorname{Cov}(Y_s,Y_t)
=
\sum_{\tau=-(L-1)}^{L-1} (L-|\tau|)\Gamma(\tau).
\]
Under summability of \(\Gamma(\tau)\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(N) = L\Sigma + o(L).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mathbb E[NN^\top] = L^2 pp^\top + L\Sigma + o(L).
\]

So the raw second moment splits into a trivial mean piece \(L^2pp^\top\) and a nontrivial fluctuation piece \(L\Sigma\). The latter is the meaningful second-order object for large-window statistics.

It is useful to separate
\[
\Sigma = \Gamma(0) + \sum_{\tau\neq 0}\Gamma(\tau),
\]
where
\[
\Gamma(0) = \operatorname{diag}(p) - pp^\top.
\]
The \(\tau=0\) term is present even for an i.i.d.\ process; it is just the multinomial baseline. Dependence across positions enters through the \(\tau\neq 0\) terms. Depending on the application, one may work with the full \(\Sigma\) or with a dependence-only or normalized variant.

\section{Embeddings from covariance factorization}

Let
\[
\Sigma = U_r \Lambda_r U_r^\top
\]
be the eigendecomposition over the positive eigenspace, with
\[
\Lambda_r = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r),
\qquad
\lambda_a > 0,
\qquad
r = \operatorname{rank}(\Sigma)\leq V-1.
\]

Define
\[
E = U_r \Lambda_r^{1/2}.
\]
If \(e_i^\top\) denotes the \(i\)-th row of \(E\), then
\[
\Sigma_{ij} = e_i \cdot e_j.
\]

Hence the rows \(e_i\) define an exact second-order embedding of tokens: inner products between token vectors reproduce the long-run covariance of count fluctuations.

This is the canonical embedding implied by the Gaussian theory, up to the usual orthogonal freedom in factorizing a positive semidefinite matrix.

Low dimensionality, however, is not automatic. It emerges only if \(\Sigma\) is approximately low-rank. If the spectrum is concentrated, one may truncate to the top \(k\) modes:
\[
\Sigma \approx U_k \Lambda_k U_k^\top = E_k E_k^\top,
\qquad
E_k = U_k \Lambda_k^{1/2}.
\]
This gives the optimal rank-\(k\) approximation to \(\Sigma\) in the usual PCA sense.

\section{Gaussian effective theory and additive features}

Because \(\Sigma\) is singular, the Gaussian density is most naturally written on \(H\):
\[
P(X) \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac12 X^\top \Sigma^+ X\right),
\qquad
X\in H,
\]
where \(\Sigma^+\) is the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse.

Let \(u_a\) be the eigenvectors corresponding to the positive eigenvalues \(\lambda_a\). Define the whitened principal coordinates
\[
q_a = \frac{u_a^\top X}{\sqrt{\lambda_a}},
\qquad
a=1,\dots,r.
\]
Then asymptotically
\[
q \Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,I_r),
\qquad
P(q) \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac12 \sum_{a=1}^r q_a^2\right).
\]

Since \(X = (N-Lp)/\sqrt L\), each \(q_a\) can be written as an additive sum over token features:
\[
q_a = \frac{1}{\sqrt L}\sum_{t=1}^L \psi_a(w_t),
\]
with
\[
\psi_a(i)
=
\frac{u_a(i)-\sum_j p_j u_a(j)}{\sqrt{\lambda_a}}.
\]

So the Gaussian theory depends on the window only through additive sums of centered, whitened token features. This is the precise sense in which embeddings linearize the leading-order statistics of large contexts.

There are therefore two equivalent viewpoints:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the covariance embedding \(e_i\), defined by \(\Sigma = EE^\top\);
\item the whitened feature embedding \(\psi(i)\), whose window sums are asymptotically standard Gaussian.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Relation to PCA, SVD, and standard co-occurrence methods}

Empirically, \(\Sigma\) is estimated by taking many windows of length \(L\), centering their count vectors, and computing the sample covariance. Diagonalizing that covariance is exactly PCA on window-count data. Equivalently, if one stacks centered window count vectors into a matrix, then the right singular vectors recover the principal eigenspaces of the empirical \(\Sigma\).

So this framework gives a direct large-window justification for PCA/SVD-type embeddings.

It is important, however, not to conflate this with arbitrary co-occurrence or PMI matrices. Those are different statistics. In particular, for an i.i.d.\ token process, \(\Sigma\) is still nonzero because of the baseline term
\[
\Gamma(0) = \operatorname{diag}(p) - pp^\top,
\]
while nonzero-lag PMI vanishes. Thus diagonalizing \(\Sigma\) is not, in general, the same as factorizing PMI.

Conceptually, this construction is closer to PCA/LSA on large bag-of-words windows than to SGNS- or GloVe-style shifted-PMI factorizations.

\section{Why the effective theory looks all-to-all}

The Gaussian approximation for counts can be written as
\[
P(N)\approx
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{1}{2L}(N-Lp)^\top \Sigma^+(N-Lp)
\right].
\]
Substituting \(N_i = \sum_{t=1}^L \mathbf 1_{\{w_t=i\}}\) yields an effective interaction among all positions in the window:
\[
-\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{s,t=1}^L K(w_s,w_t),
\]
for a kernel \(K\) determined by \(\Sigma^+\), up to one-body and constant terms.

So the coarse-grained description is indeed all-to-all across the window, with mean-field scaling \(1/L\). But that all-to-all form is not what produces Gaussianity. Gaussianity comes from the CLT: the count vector is a sum of many weakly dependent contributions, and under short-range dependence only the second cumulant survives at leading order after \(\sqrt L\) rescaling.

\section{What this description captures --- and what it misses}

At the Gaussian level, the theory captures:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the full leading-order covariance of large-window bag-of-words fluctuations;
\item the dominant collective modes in which token counts co-vary;
\item the best low-rank second-order approximation obtained by spectral truncation.
\end{enumerate}

What it does \emph{not} capture includes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item ordering and positional structure inside the window;
\item higher cumulants of the count distribution;
\item nonlinear or multi-token interactions not reducible to second moments;
\item non-quadratic large-deviation behavior.
\end{enumerate}

For short-range dependent processes, the \(m\)-th cumulant of the raw count vector typically scales as \(O(L)\). Therefore the \(m\)-th cumulant of
\[
X_L = \frac{N-Lp}{\sqrt L}
\]
scales as
\[
O\!\left(L^{1-m/2}\right).
\]
So third-order corrections are \(O(L^{-1/2})\), fourth-order corrections are \(O(L^{-1})\), and so on. This is why the Gaussian theory is asymptotically exact in the central regime, and also why non-Gaussian structure can be organized systematically as a correction expansion.

\section{Takeaway}

In the large-window limit, the centered token-count vector satisfies a multivariate CLT and is therefore governed, to leading order, by a Gaussian law with covariance \(\Sigma\), the long-run covariance of the one-hot token process. A factorization
\[
\Sigma = EE^\top
\]
then produces a canonical second-order embedding of tokens, and truncation to the leading eigenspaces gives the corresponding optimal low-rank representation.

This makes embeddings the natural first representation for large-context bag-of-words statistics: they are simply coordinates for the dominant Gaussian fluctuation modes. Everything beyond that --- ordering, higher-order interactions, and genuinely non-Gaussian structure --- lies in the correction to this leading theory.

\section{Exact realization by a permutation-invariant architecture}

It is useful to make the preceding Gaussian picture constructive. Suppose the window length \(L\) is fixed, and suppose the probability of a window depends only on its count vector
\[
N=(N_1,\dots,N_V)^\top,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^V N_i=L.
\]
Assume further that this dependence is exactly quadratic in the counts, i.e.\ of Gaussian form on the count hyperplane. Then the corresponding probability law can be represented exactly by a permutation-invariant architecture of the form
\[
\text{embedding lookup}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{sum pooling}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{quadratic energy head}.
\]
In other words, no order-sensitive mechanism is needed: for this class of distributions, the sufficient statistic is simply the sum of token embeddings across the window.

\subsection{Quadratic laws on count vectors}

Let
\[
H=\{x\in\mathbb R^V:\mathbf 1^\top x=0\}
\]
be the count-fluctuation hyperplane. A general centered Gaussian-type energy on fixed-length windows has the form
\[
\mathcal E(N)
=
\frac12 (N-\mu)^\top A (N-\mu),
\]
where
\[
\mathbf 1^\top \mu = L,
\qquad
A\succeq 0 \;\; \text{on } H,
\qquad
A\mathbf 1 = 0.
\]
The corresponding permutation-invariant sequence model is
\[
p(w_{1:L}) \propto \exp\!\bigl(-\mathcal E(N(w_{1:L}))\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, one may view this as a discrete Gaussian on the lattice
\[
\{N\in \mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^V:\mathbf 1^\top N=L\}.
\]

\qquad
E = U_r \Lambda_r^{1/2},
\]
with \(r=\operatorname{rank}(\Sigma)\), and let \(e_i^\top\) denote the \(i\)-th row of \(E\).

\[
s
=
\sum_{t=1}^L e_{w_t}
=
E^\top N.
\]
This quantity is permutation invariant by construction.

The key point is that \(s\) contains exactly the same information as \(N\) along the support directions of the Gaussian theory. Indeed, on the support subspace
\[
\operatorname{Im}(\Sigma)\subseteq H,
\]
the map
\[
x \mapsto E^\top x
\]
is injective. Hence any quadratic function of \(N\) restricted to the Gaussian support can be rewritten as a quadratic function of \(s\).

More explicitly, if the model is supported on \(\mu+\operatorname{Im}(\Sigma)\), then there exist a positive semidefinite matrix \(M\), a vector \(d\), and a constant \(c\) such that
\[
\mathcal E(N)
=
\frac12 (s-d)^\top M (s-d) + c.
\]
Therefore the exact log-probability can be written as
\[
\log p(w_{1:L})
=
c' - \frac12 (s-d)^\top M (s-d),
\qquad
s=\sum_{t=1}^L e_{w_t}.
\]

This already identifies the needed architecture: it is a \emph{Deep Sets} model with a quadratic readout.

\subsection{The exact architecture}

A convenient parameterization is
\[
x_t = e_{w_t}\in\mathbb R^r,
\qquad
s = \sum_{t=1}^L x_t,
\qquad
h = Ws+b,
\]
followed by the scalar energy
\[
\log p_\theta(w_{1:L})
=
c - \frac12 \|h\|^2.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\log p_\theta(w_{1:L})
=
c - \frac12 (Ws+b)^\top (Ws+b).
\]

This architecture is permutation invariant because it depends on the tokens only through the pooled sum \(s\). It is also exactly expressive for the quadratic family above, provided the full embedding space is used.

Thus, for an exactly Gaussian law on token counts, the required architecture is:
\[
\boxed{
\text{token embedding lookup}
\;\to\;
\text{sum pooling}
\;\to\;
\text{linear map}
\;\to\;
\text{squared norm}
}
\]
and nothing more elaborate is needed.

\subsection{Specialization to the Gaussian effective theory from \(\Sigma\)}

For the specific Gaussian theory derived earlier,
\[
p(w_{1:L})
\propto
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{1}{2L}(N-Lp)^\top \Sigma^+(N-Lp)
\right],
\]
the quadratic head can be written in closed form in the covariance embedding coordinates.

Let
\[
E = U_r \Lambda_r^{1/2},
\qquad
s = E^\top N,
\qquad
\bar s = E^\top p.
\]
Then
\[
s - L\bar s = E^\top (N-Lp),
\]
and the Gaussian energy becomes
\[
\frac{1}{2L}(N-Lp)^\top \Sigma^+(N-Lp)
=
\frac{1}{2L}(s-L\bar s)^\top \Lambda_r^{-2}(s-L\bar s).
\]
Hence an exact realization is obtained by choosing
\[
h = L^{-1/2}\Lambda_r^{-1}(s-L\bar s),
\]
so that
\[
\log p(w_{1:L})
=
c - \frac12 \|h\|^2.
\]

Therefore the Gaussian effective theory from the previous sections is not merely approximable by such a model; it is exactly of this form.

\subsection{Whitened additive features}

The same construction becomes even simpler in the whitened basis introduced earlier. Define

\psi(i)=\bigl(\psi_a(i)\bigr)_{a=1}^r,

\psi_a(i)=
\frac{u_a(i)-\sum_j p_j u_a(j)}{\sqrt{\lambda_a}}.
\]
Then
\[
q
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt L}\sum_{t=1}^L \psi(w_t)
\]
has asymptotically standard Gaussian statistics, and for the exact quadratic model above one has
\[
\log p(w_{1:L})
=
c - \frac12 \|q\|^2.
\]
In that basis, the network is simply
\[
\text{lookup whitened feature}
\;\to\;
\text{sum}
\;\to\;
\text{squared norm}.
\]

\subsection{Learning from data}

If the embeddings are fixed in advance, then the only learnable part of the model is the quadratic head:
\[
\log p_\theta(w_{1:L})
=
c - \frac12 (Ws+b)^\top (Ws+b).
\]
This is a finite-dimensional parametric family, and under correct specification standard maximum-likelihood arguments give consistency as the number of sampled windows grows.

If the embeddings are learned jointly with the head, the same architecture remains expressive enough, but the parameterization is no longer unique. There is a natural gauge freedom:
\[
e_i \mapsto R e_i,
\qquad
W \mapsto W R^{-1},
\]
for any invertible change of latent basis \(R\). Thus the learned embedding coordinates are identifiable only up to such transformations, exactly as one would expect from a covariance factorization.

For the exact Gaussian setting, deeper set encoders, attention, recurrence, or positional structure are unnecessary. Those only become relevant once one moves beyond permutation-invariant quadratic laws.

\subsection{Sequence probabilities versus count probabilities}

A final bookkeeping point is worth making explicit. There are two distinct objects one might call a ``Gaussian law on frequencies'':

\begin{enumerate}
\item a Gaussian-type law on \emph{sequences}, in which
\[
p(w_{1:L}) \propto \exp\!\bigl(-\mathcal E(N(w_{1:L}))\bigr);
\]

\item a Gaussian-type law on \emph{count vectors}, in which
\[
p_{\mathrm{count}}(N) \propto \exp\!\bigl(-\mathcal E(N)\bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}

These differ by the multinomial multiplicity factor
\[
\#\{w_{1:L}:N(w_{1:L})=N\}
=
\frac{L!}{\prod_i N_i!}.
\]
Accordingly,
\[
p_{\mathrm{count}}(N)
=
\frac{L!}{\prod_i N_i!}\, p_{\mathrm{seq}}(w_{1:L})
\]
for any sequence \(w_{1:L}\) with count vector \(N\).

The architecture above directly models the permutation-invariant \emph{sequence} probability. If one instead wishes to model the count-vector law itself, then this combinatorial factor must be included separately.

\subsection{Takeaway}

For a fixed-length window and a permutation-invariant probability law that is exactly quadratic in token counts, the correct architecture is a permutation-invariant energy-based model with sum pooling and a quadratic head. Using the full covariance-factorization embedding \(e_i\), the pooled sum
\[
s=\sum_{t=1}^L e_{w_t}
\]
is already a sufficient statistic for the Gaussian support directions, and the exact probability takes the form
\[
\log p(w_{1:L})
=
c - \frac12 (Ws+b)^\top (Ws+b).
\]
Thus the Gaussian count model is not merely compatible with embedding-based learning; it is exactly realized by the simplest possible set architecture.

\section{Appendix: exact realization by a Deep Sets architecture}

This appendix records the exact architecture needed to realize the Gaussian count model from the main text.

Let
\[
\Sigma = U_r \Lambda_r U_r^\top,
\qquad
E = U_r \Lambda_r^{1/2},
\]
with \(r=\operatorname{rank}(\Sigma)\), and let \(e_i^\top\) denote the \(i\)-th row of \(E\). For a window \(w_{1:L}\), write
\[
N = (N_1,\dots,N_V)^\top,
\qquad
s = \sum_{t=1}^L e_{w_t} = E^\top N.
\]

\paragraph{Lemma.}
The pooled embedding sum \(s\) determines the count fluctuation \(N-Lp\) along the Gaussian support directions. More precisely, if \(x\in \operatorname{Im}(\Sigma)\) and \(E^\top x=0\), then \(x=0\).

\begin{proof}
Write \(x=U_r y\) for some \(y\in\mathbb R^r\). Then
\[
E^\top x
=
\Lambda_r^{1/2} U_r^\top U_r y
=
\Lambda_r^{1/2} y.
\]
If \(E^\top x=0\), then \(\Lambda_r^{1/2}y=0\). Since \(\Lambda_r\) is positive definite on the retained eigenspace, \(y=0\), hence \(x=0\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proposition.}
Consider the Gaussian effective theory
\[
p(w_{1:L})
\propto
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{1}{2L}(N-Lp)^\top \Sigma^+(N-Lp)
\right].
\]
Then this law is realized exactly by a permutation-invariant architecture of the form
\[
h=\sum_{t=1}^L \phi(w_t),
\qquad
\log p(w_{1:L}) = c-\frac12\|h\|^2,
\]
with
\[
\phi(i)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt L}\Lambda_r^{-1}\bigl(e_i-E^\top p\bigr).
\]

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
x := N-Lp \in \operatorname{Im}(\Sigma),
\qquad
z := s-LE^\top p = E^\top x.
\]
Writing \(x=U_r y\), we have
\[
z
=
E^\top x
=
\Lambda_r^{1/2} y,
\qquad
y=\Lambda_r^{-1/2} z.
\]
Hence
\[
x^\top \Sigma^+ x
=
y^\top \Lambda_r^{-1} y
=
z^\top \Lambda_r^{-2} z.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{1}{2L}(N-Lp)^\top \Sigma^+(N-Lp)
=
\frac12
\left\|
\frac{1}{\sqrt L}\Lambda_r^{-1}(s-LE^\top p)
\right\|^2.
\]
Thus the log-probability takes the form
\[
\log p(w_{1:L})
=
c-\frac12\|h\|^2,
\qquad
h=
\frac{1}{\sqrt L}\Lambda_r^{-1}(s-LE^\top p).
\]
Since
\[
h
=
\sum_{t=1}^L
\frac{1}{\sqrt L}\Lambda_r^{-1}\bigl(e_{w_t}-E^\top p\bigr),
\]

\[
\phi(i)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt L}\Lambda_r^{-1}\bigl(e_i-E^\top p\bigr).
\]
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Corollary.}
More generally, let
\[
p(w_{1:L})
\propto
\exp\!\left[
-\frac12 (N-\mu)^\top A (N-\mu)
\right],
\]
where \(N-\mu\in \operatorname{Im}(\Sigma)\) almost surely and \(A\) is positive semidefinite on \(\operatorname{Im}(\Sigma)\). Then there exist \(M\succeq 0\), \(d\), and \(c\) such that
\[
\log p(w_{1:L})
=
c-\frac12 (s-d)^\top M (s-d),
\qquad
s=\sum_{t=1}^L e_{w_t}.
\]

\begin{proof}
On \(\operatorname{Im}(\Sigma)\), write
\[
A = U_r B U_r^\top
\]
for some \(B\succeq 0\). Let
\[
x := N-\mu = U_r y,
\qquad
z := s-E^\top\mu = E^\top x = \Lambda_r^{1/2} y.
\]
Then
\[
y=\Lambda_r^{-1/2}z,
\]
and therefore
\[
x^\top A x
=
y^\top B y
=
z^\top \Lambda_r^{-1/2} B \Lambda_r^{-1/2} z.
\]
So the quadratic energy can be rewritten as
\[
\frac12 (s-d)^\top M (s-d)
\]
with
\[
d=E^\top\mu,
\qquad
M=\Lambda_r^{-1/2} B \Lambda_r^{-1/2}.
\]
\end{proof}

\end{enumerate}

6. Minimal pseudocode
Using the exact hard-coded version:

# token ids w: shape (L,)
# E: (V, r)
# Lambda_inv: (r,) or diagonal matrix
# e_bar = E.T @ p: shape (r,)
